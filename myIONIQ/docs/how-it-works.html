How It Works

Where each number comes from and how fresh it is.

Data Sources

Bluelink is the source of truth for remote commands and the fallback for everything else. But Bluelink usually serves the status the car last uploaded, which can be hours old. So when the OBD-II adapter has a newer reading, the app uses it for state of charge, charging, charge power and locks.

The rule is simply the newest reading wins. Range always comes from Bluelink, because the car doesn't report range over OBD.

Status Line

The line under the range figure tells you how current the screen is:

Parked · 6h ago with an OBD port markThe newest reading came from the adapter, the last time you were with the car.

Widgets and the Apple Watch show the same age and source.

Refreshing

  • When you open the app, it fetches from Bluelink if the last status is older than your Background Refresh interval.
  • In the background, Settings › Background Refresh sets an interval (four hours by default) and the hours it's allowed to run. iOS decides the exact timing.
  • By hand, the refresh button asks the car itself for a fresh status. Bluelink limits how often that can happen, so the app warns you after three manual refreshes in an hour.

If Bluelink says the car is busy with another request, the app waits and retries after 15 seconds, then 30. Commands sent while your phone is offline are queued and sent when it reconnects.

Charge History

Bluelink doesn't keep a charge history, so myIONIQ builds its own:

  • With the OBD-II adapter connected, it records the charge every minute, including real charge power from the charger's voltage and current.
  • Readings become sessions with a power curve, energy added, time taken and peak power.
  • A charge that happened while the app wasn't watching is still filed, inferred from the readings before and after it.
  • A session stays open if you walk away from the car partway through, and closes once the readings show it has ended.
  • While the adapter is live, Bluelink's charging flag is ignored, so a stale "not charging" can't cut a session short.

Trips

Bluelink in Canada returns no trip data, so myIONIQ records drives on your iPhone. A trip starts when you set off, using CarPlay, the adapter or motion to tell the car is in use, and is filed when the car switches off. With the adapter connected, trips include energy used and regen from the battery's own counters.

CarPlay

  • Routing comes from Mapbox, with live traffic, lane guidance and signals.
  • Charging stops come from Open Charge Map. Each stop charges to what the next leg needs, capped at your maximum charge, rather than a flat 80%.
  • Favourite Routes learns the way you like to drive somewhere and offers it first. Suggest Destinations guesses where you're heading when you get in. Both learn on your iPhone.
  • Send to Vehicle on the phone's Navigate screen hands a destination to CarPlay, together with the charging stops you planned, so the car drives the plan you looked at.
  • In a dead zone, a route request waits for a connection instead of reporting that no route was found.

Cluster and HUD Directions

The IONIQ 6 cluster and HUD draw guidance from a handful of CAN frames the head unit normally sends for its own navigation. I decoded them from recordings and parked tests: regular frames carry the arrow, distance and countdown bar, and three longer messages carry the street name, the HUD's list of upcoming turns and an 81-byte lane graphic.

During a myIONIQ route, the phone sends what to show over Bluetooth, and the harness firmware slots it into the car's traffic. If the head unit draws and then clears its own lanes near an interchange, the firmware puts the app's lanes straight back.

Live Activities

iOS rarely lets an app run in the background, so a small server sends a silent push at intervals during a charge. Each push wakes the app long enough to refresh from Bluelink and update the Live Activity. The pushes pause while the OBD-II adapter is connected, since it already has fresher numbers.

Widgets, Watch and Shortcuts

  • Widgets show the app's latest snapshot and refresh whenever it changes. While the adapter is connected with nothing changing, their "updated" time still moves forward every 15 minutes.
  • The Apple Watch app mirrors the phone's status and sends commands through the phone.
  • Shortcuts can lock or unlock the car, check its battery, charging, locks and climate, find where it's parked, or plan a trip, from Siri or an automation.
  • Walk-away locking uses a Bluetooth device in the car to tell when you've walked away, then locks the car and tells you it did. Set it up in Settings › Auto-Lock.

Your Data

  • Your Bluelink email, password and PIN are stored only in your iPhone's Keychain and are only ever sent to Bluelink.
  • Charge history, trips, learned routes and saved places stay on your iPhone.
  • The wake-up server only stores what it needs to send a push to your phone during a charge, never your Bluelink credentials.
  • A bug report sends what you write, plus a short summary of the app's settings and state to help me reproduce it.
